Part 1 · A $1,000 wheel · 00:00–02:23 ▶ watch on YouTube
Listen for: a wheel of cheeseit costs over … per poundmore than double the price ofage / be ageda big businessbe worth an eye-popping …one of the biggest exportsa restricted areacertified dairies
  1. How much can one wheel of parmesan cost, and how much does it weigh? Over $1,000; the average wheel weighs 88 pounds (about 40 kg), so over $11 per pound.
  2. How does its price compare with cheddar? It’s more than double the price of average cheddar.
  3. How big is the parmesan business for Italy? About 3.6 million wheels a year; the industry is worth €2.2 billion — one of Italy’s biggest exports.
  4. Where can it be made, and why only there? Only in a restricted area of northern Italy (Emilia-Romagna); 329 certified dairies. It’s about three good bacteria that grow only in this territory.
  5. What are the only three ingredients? Cow’s milk, salt and rennet.
Part 2 · Mark of origin, maturation and price · 06:11–07:58 ▶ watch on YouTube
Listen for: a mark of originthe real thinga master graderinspect / brand the wheelsmaturationa stronger, nuttier tastemake the price go higher
  1. What does the stencil on the wheel show customers? The mark of origin — that this is the real thing from the restricted area.
  2. What information is on the ID plaque? Where and when the cheese was produced.
  3. What happens after one year? A master grader inspects the wheels and brands them.
  4. Why does a longer maturation make the price higher? Older cheese (2 years on average, up to 10) has a stronger, nuttier taste; three-year-old ‘vecchio’ costs about €13 per pound.
Part 3 · Counterfeits and how to spot the real thing · 07:58–09:46 ▶ watch on YouTube
Listen for: a black marketcounterfeit productsfakesfight copycatsprotected designation of originget the recognition it deserveslegitthe official logo
  1. What problem does the high price create? A black market of counterfeit parmesan products.
  2. How big is the fake-food industry in Italy? Estimated at about €100 billion.
  3. Where is parmesan protected, and where is it not? It is protected in the EU as a PDO, but it struggles to get recognition in the rest of the world.
  4. How can a shopper recognise real parmesan? The dotted stencil on the rind, or the official logo and the PDO mark on the pack.
Case file: research on cheese buyers
  • Award stickers vs taste descriptions. Oregon State University showed 488 cheese buyers pictures of familiar cheeses (brie, cheddar) and unfamiliar ones (Coulommiers, Cantal), with prices, award stickers or short taste descriptions. Shoppers who like trying new food paid more for unfamiliar cheese with an award sticker. Shoppers who prefer familiar cheese cared more about the taste description.
  • What shoppers buy. In a survey of 1,821 people in Canada and the US, more than a third preferred hard or firm cheese. Most bought pre-packed blocks (91%), shredded (80%) or sliced cheese (79%). For more than 80%, local production was not an important factor.
  • Ukraine. Ukrainian cheese production fell to 57,000 tonnes in 2024 — about 20% less than in 2023 — because of lower milk yields and fewer cows. Exports were about 6,000 tonnes, around 11% of production.

Read the case file. What would you test first for a Ukrainian hard cheese: an award sticker, a taste description, the pack format or the price?

    ✓ Tell me about the last time you bought hard cheese. Which one did you choose and why?
