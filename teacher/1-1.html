Cheat sheet: how to talk about market research in dairyШпаргалка: як говорити про дослідження ринку в молочці

The main idea: a new product, flavour or pack should be based on data about shoppers and retail partners, not on the opinion of the boss. In dairy and FMCG, research means taste tests, retail sales data, store checks, complaints and talking to category managers.Головна думка: новий продукт, смак чи упаковка мають спиратися на дані про покупців і торговельних партнерів, а не на думку керівника. У молочці та FMCG дослідження — це дегустації, дані продажів мереж, перевірки в магазинах, скарги і розмови з категорійними менеджерами.

Answers and model answersВідповіді та зразки

10A — model (Halychyna vs Yahotynske kefir)

Both brands attract customers with a familiar, traditional product; their target audience is families who buy dairy several times a week. The promise is freshness and a natural taste, so shelf life and customer satisfaction are key. Both are formidable competitors because they get good shelf space in big chains. For the ideal customer the main pain point is trust in quality, and the price point must stay affordable.

10B — better questions

  1. Tell me about the last time you bought kefir. Which one did you choose and why?
  2. What do you look at first when you choose yoghurt on the shelf?
  3. Which flavours have you tried recently? What didn’t you like about them?
  4. How much do you usually pay for milk? When do you choose a more expensive one? (the question tag in the original biases the answer)

Good survey (high-protein kefir): 1 How often do you buy kefir? (multiple-choice, quant.) · 2 Do you check the protein content on the label? (yes/no, quant.) · 3 How satisfied are you with the kefir you buy now, 1–10? (rating, quant.) · 4 Tell us about the last time you looked for a high-protein snack. (open-ended, qual.) · 5 Where do you usually buy dairy? (multiple-choice) · 6 What don’t you like about high-protein products you’ve tried? (open-ended, qual.)

10D — model script

Our target audience is people aged 25–40 who go to the gym. Over half of the respondents admitted that they buy protein snacks every week, and several raised concerns that these products contain too much sugar. The burning problem is a healthy, affordable protein snack, so it warrants a new product. As a next step, we’ll run a blind taste test with 100 shoppers and a test launch in ten stores to get hard data.

★ Challenges — model replies

  • Hard data or compliments? — Not compliments: 60 of 100 shoppers chose our sample in the blind tasting.
  • Fifteen people isn’t a sample size. — Right, that was a pilot. Next we’ll test with 100 shoppers in three cities.
  • Why should a chain give us shelf space? — Because the category is growing and we’ll support the launch with promotions and tastings in store.
  • Shoppers won’t pay more. — Our price point is between the standard and the premium kefir — like Chobani’s strategy.
  • Our competitor is formidable. — Yes, so we won’t copy them; we focus on a pain point they ignore: sugar.
